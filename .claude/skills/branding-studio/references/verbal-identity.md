# Verbal identity — turn truth into generative language

Use when proposition, messaging, voice/tone or a broader verbal system is materially in scope. Keep truth and evidence upstream; this reference governs how valid meaning becomes usable language.

## 1. Separate proposition, message and expression

Do not let tone or tagline impersonate strategy.

- **proposition** — what the brand can legitimately claim and why it matters;
- **proof** — evidence capable of supporting important claims;
- **message** — what this audience/context needs to understand now and what should happen next;
- **expression** — how this brand says it here.

A tagline may express a proposition; it is not required. Personality labels may summarize behavior; they are not the behavior itself.

## 2. Build a verbal grammar that changes writing decisions

Define only principles that cause observable choices in real copy. Depending on the brand, useful variables can include:
- directness versus indirection;
- concrete versus abstract language;
- sentence length and rhythm;
- terminology and level of technicality;
- pronoun and point-of-view behavior;
- use or avoidance of humor, metaphor, questions or commands;
- how evidence, uncertainty and calls to action are expressed;
- meaningful exclusions that prevent category-autocomplete language.

Replace generic adjectives such as `human`, `bold`, `authentic` or `confident` with the writing decisions they actually require. If a principle would not change a sentence, it is not useful guidance.

## 3. Modulate by audience, channel and moment

Preserve the underlying truth while changing emphasis and expression when context changes.

Do not force one wording across homepage, product UI, support, executive communication, campaign and long-form content merely to look consistent. Coherence comes from the same governing verbal logic, not identical phrasing.

Use message hierarchy to decide what must be understood first, what needs proof and what can remain secondary. More key messages do not create better architecture.

## 4. Treat existing language as evidence in established brands

When a brand already exists, inspect actual language in product, site, support, sales, social and other relevant touchpoints before replacing it. Absence of verbal guidelines is not evidence that no verbal identity exists.

Preserve useful equity and recurring language behavior when it still serves the brand job; change only what the problem requires.

## 5. Keep naming a related but separate decision

When naming is in scope, also load `naming.md`. Naming has semantic, phonetic, operational, linguistic and legal consequences that voice/tone guidance does not resolve.

A name can influence verbal and visual expression, and design can reveal naming problems, so iteration is legitimate. Do not duplicate clearance or linguistic-risk rules here.

## 6. Let verbal and visual expression divide the work

Words and visuals do not need to restate one another. Decide which medium should carry information, emotion, specificity or surprise in each touchpoint while preserving one governing idea.

Redundant explanation is not coherence; contradiction without intent is not range.

## 7. Test generativity in real communication

Use representative communication moments with different jobs and audiences. Generate new copy from the principles rather than copying supplied examples.

Ask:
- Is the central claim actually true and appropriately supported?
- Does each message emphasize what matters in that context?
- Do the verbal principles produce meaningfully different writing decisions?
- Can expression adapt without changing the underlying truth?
- Could another competent writer create a new, correct communication from the guidance alone?
- Would swapping the brand name for a generic competitor leave most of the language equally valid? If so, specificity is weak.

Examples are decoding aids. If operators can only imitate the examples, the verbal grammar is under-resolved.
