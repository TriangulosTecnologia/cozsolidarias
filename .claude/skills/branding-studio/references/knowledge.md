# Knowledge calibration

Match claim strength to evidence. Use branding frameworks as tools, not laws, and do not let professional convention impersonate empirical proof.

## Robust operating principles

Treat these as broad working principles:
- strategy and real context should constrain consequential identity decisions;
- identity is broader than a logo and operates through verbal, visual and experiential cues;
- recognizable systems need both coherence and enough flexibility to work across real touchpoints;
- unnecessary change can destroy accumulated equity;
- representative applications reveal failures that isolated identity boards cannot;
- actual perception/reputation exists outside the spec and must be evidenced externally when claimed;
- accessibility, legibility, legal and production constraints can materially affect identity performance.

## Established practices, not universal requirements

Use when they improve the current decision:
- competitive/category audits;
- positioning or brand briefs;
- naming pipelines with linguistic/legal triage;
- tone-by-context guidance;
- typographic, color, composition and image systems;
- clear-space/minimum-size/monochrome variants when actual production requires them;
- brand guidelines/centers;
- trial applications;
- migration/versioning during rebrands.

Do not include one merely because branding projects often do.

## Empirical/context-dependent ideas

Use directionally and preserve scope limits:
- distinctive assets can differ materially in recognition/fame/uniqueness; do not assume a cue is distinctive because it was intentionally designed that way;
- mental availability/category-entry-point frameworks can be useful in contexts where the market/research model fits, but are not mandatory strategy fields;
- sound symbolism/phonosemantics can offer modest contextual signals and should not override meaning, language reality or legal risk;
- OKLab/OKLCH are useful perceptual color spaces for technical manipulation/comparison.

## Contested or weak as universal rules

Do not present as law:
- brand archetypes;
- universal color psychology (`blue = trust`, etc.);
- golden-ratio explanations of logo quality;
- fixed formulas for concept count, font count, color count or grid units;
- “minimal geometric sans = modern/professional”;
- “serif = premium/trustworthy”;
- novelty, complexity or unusual layout as proof of creativity/sophistication;
- precise numerical creative-quality scores without validated measurement.

## Current / legal facts

Verify current authoritative sources when materially relevant:
- trademark procedures, fees, timelines and filing rules;
- classification interpretation for a specific filing;
- current domain/handle availability;
- jurisdiction-specific accessibility/legal obligations;
- current competitor/product facts.

Trademark searching by the agent is triage, not definitive legal clearance. Escalate to qualified counsel when consequence justifies it.

## Counterfeit-quality warnings

Do not use:
- a complete spec as proof that the brand is resolved;
- a clever symbol story as proof of meaning;
- a fashionable or category-coded style as proof of distinctiveness, premium quality or timelessness;
- repetition as proof of coherence or recognizability;
- mockup polish as proof that a system works in real content/touchpoints;
- research volume as proof of insight;
- model agreement/self-critique as proof of market perception;
- structural JSON validity as proof of strategy, direction or craft;
- a generated image looking original as proof of legal uniqueness.

The surface mechanism may still be correct when the brief independently earns it.

## Accessibility notes

WCAG 2.x contrast criteria are a widely used digital accessibility baseline. Do not describe them as a universal legal floor; applicable law varies by jurisdiction and product context.

APCA/Lc can be used as an additional perceptual contrast signal where useful, but should not be presented as universally adopted compliance.

## Boundaries

- **Real perception / recall / fame / preference / behavioral effect** → external evidence.
- **Definitive trademark clearance** → appropriate legal review.
- **Production master** → reproducible craft/tooling and applicable checks.
- **Creative quality** → professional judgment plus artifact/context evidence, not a deterministic validator.
- **Stakeholder preference** → legitimate preference/authority when relevant, but not disguised as market evidence.
