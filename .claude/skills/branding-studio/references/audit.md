# AUDIT — evaluate an artifact or brand system

Use AUDIT when an existing artifact/system must be judged against the current brand contract and its actual job.

Without a usable contract, do not present a standards-based compliance audit. Reconstruct the relevant brand rules first or provide an explicitly professional critique without false compliance language.

## 1. Inspect the richest available source

Prefer native/structured source over screenshots when available: source design files, SVG, HTML/CSS, PPTX, documents, code/components or other inspectable artifacts.

Use screenshots/renders for visible/contextual behavior. Do not present estimated values as exact source facts.

Compile only the contract subset relevant to the artifact's job, audience, medium and moment.

## 2. Separate what each form of evidence can establish

Keep findings distinguishable:
- **exact/source** — values, files, tokens, dimensions, master integrity and other machine/source-checkable properties;
- **professional judgment** — hierarchy, strategic/creative fit, recognizability, composition, voice, craft and coherence;
- **contextual artifact evidence** — what happens when the work is rendered/used at representative size, density, environment or sequence;
- **external reality** — actual perception, behavior, recall, stakeholder response, legal status or other outside-world outcomes.

Do not hide judgment behind pseudo-objective decimals. A rubric can structure comparison; it does not convert taste or causal uncertainty into measurement.

## 3. Audit against the quality target

Check the relevant subset of:
- truth;
- relevance to the artifact/brand job;
- specificity to this brand;
- perceptible distinction;
- coherence across verbal/visual behavior;
- generativity / whether this is a valid expression rather than template copying;
- flexibility where range matters;
- craft;
- real contextual performance;
- durability vs fashionable signaling.

For a system-level audit, test multiple materially different applications rather than judging an identity board in isolation.

## 4. Falsify counterfeit quality

Look specifically for:
- complete-looking guidelines masking weak decisions;
- logo symbolism standing in for meaning;
- category-coded styling presented as distinctiveness;
- repetition presented as coherence;
- mockup polish masking poor real-content performance;
- novelty/complexity presented as sophistication;
- research volume presented as insight.

Do not penalize a familiar device merely because it is familiar. Ask whether this brief earns it and whether it actually produces the claimed property.

## 5. Issue a practical verdict

Use the minimum language necessary:
- **PASS** — no material defect found for the requested scope;
- **PASS WITH RESERVATIONS** — usable, but material improvements or evidence gaps remain;
- **FAIL** — clear material defect in the requested scope;
- **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE** — the requested claim cannot responsibly be made from available evidence.

Prioritize by consequence:
1. artifact/job failure;
2. accessibility/legal/production risk;
3. strategy/brand-system integrity;
4. specificity/distinctive behavior;
5. craft/refinement.

## 6. Classify the correction scope

- local execution error → fix artifact;
- recurring guidance ambiguity → small system patch;
- repeated failure across legitimate applications → EVOLVE candidate;
- governing idea cannot generate the needed range → reopen creative direction;
- external evidence contradicts a strategy assumption → revisit affected strategy before redesigning downstream expression.

Do not mutate the contract because an artifact is merely unattractive or because a reviewer prefers another style.

## Report

Return:
- artifact/system job;
- evidence inspected and its limits;
- prioritized material findings;
- correction level: artifact | guidance | system | direction | strategy;
- applicable exact/contextual checks;
- unresolved external-reality gaps only when they matter.

For specialist-produced lettering, illustration, logo craft or production assets, keep “meets creative brief,” “craft quality,” “production validity” and “personal preference” separate.
